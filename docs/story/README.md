### Palaces, not Gyms

Each sibling's battle is a **Trial of Standing** fought in the throne hall of their
royal palace, in front of their court. Each palace replaces the Gym building in its town
and keeps the Gym's structure: a few courtiers or guards to beat on the way in, an
optional palace puzzle, and the sibling on the throne at the end. Winning earns the
sibling's **seal**, which is the badge. In these docs "Trial" means the Gym-leader fight
and "palace" means the Gym building.

### Boy or girl

The player chooses at the start, as in Emerald. Every line that names the player's title
needs both forms: **prince/princess**, **Your Highness** is shared, **Marsh Prince/Marsh
Princess**, **brother/sister**, **son/daughter**. The default name is *Rowan* for both.
These docs call the player "the player" and use "they".

## Tone

Political thriller first, Pokémon adventure second. People lie, bargain and posture.
Battles are the formal, legal way heirs settle disputes, so they matter politically, not
just as sport. Keep it readable for a Pokémon audience: no gore, menace stays implied,
and humour comes from courtiers and townsfolk. Every sibling should be someone the player
can understand, even the ones who never redeem themselves.

**Darker tone** (D's call, 2026-10-09): the siblings' crimes and their aftermath are
**shown on screen**, not only reported. This is darker than a typical Pokémon game, on
purpose. Limits that keep it stageable on the GBA and readable:

- No blood, no bodies on screen. Deaths may be stated plainly ("died", "killed").
- Stage violence with what the engine already does well: screen shake, flashes,
  fade-to-black at the moment of harm, sprites walking off or being led away,
  emote bubbles, weather (rain, fog), music cutting out.
- Show the aftermath: boarded houses, graves and memorials, empty beds, a dropped
  belonging, people who no longer recognise their friends.
- Each case keeps its documents and testimony as well; the on-screen scene is what makes
  it land.
