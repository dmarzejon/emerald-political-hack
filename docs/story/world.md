# World

## The Kingdom of Aldmere

A small island kingdom of ten towns around one capital. It has been ruled for three
hundred years by the House of Valcourt. Pokémon are bound up with law: every noble keeps
a battle team, and a formal Pokémon battle between nobles, a **Trial of Standing**, is a
legal way to settle a dispute. Whoever loses a Trial must accept the winner's terms.

### The Apportionment

By old custom each child of the king is given one town to govern when they come of age.
How well their town prospers is how the court judges their fitness to inherit. The
eldest usually gets the richest town, so the custom mostly reinforces the order of birth.

With the town, each heir is **bestowed a legendary Pokémon** from the Crown's reliquary,
as a sign of royal blood. The nine trueborn siblings each received one (see
[characters.md](characters.md#the-nine-siblings)). The Chancellery withheld the player's,
calling a bastard unworthy of it. That is why the king's private gift of Entei, Raikou or
Suicune matters so much: it is the bestowal the court refused.

The king has ten children. Nine are by Queen Ottilie (died 6 years before the game). The
tenth, the player, is by **Elena Marsh**, a commoner healer. The player was apportioned
**Lowmere**, the poorest town in the kingdom, as a public insult arranged by the court.

### Diplomatic war

Heirs cannot raise armies against each other; the Crown forbids it. Instead they fight by
**pacts**. An heir who wins a Trial of Standing against another heir may impose a pact on
the loser's town: trade terms, reforms, the release of prisoners, the opening of roads.
The player's campaign is a chain of these Trials. Each is fought in the throne hall of the
sibling's palace, before their court; this replaces the Gym battle. Each win
yields a pact that both reforms the sibling's town and sends something back to Lowmere,
which is how Lowmere grows (see [vertical-slice.md](vertical-slice.md#lowmere-upgrade-stages)).

To earn a Trial the player must first build a **case**: proof that the sibling's rule is
failing or corrupt, presented to the town's magistrate. This is the investigative half of
each town and the main vehicle for the thriller plot.

## The King

**King Aldric III, "the Sleeping King".** Once a fair and well-loved ruler. Since Elena's
death twelve years ago he has suffered waking nightmares and now rarely leaves his
chambers. Royal decrees still come out under his seal, written by his Chancellor. Court
gossip says he is mad. The truth is that the Pale Choir keeps him in a nightmare state
(see [cult.md](cult.md)).

Aldric loved Elena and acknowledged the player as his child, which is why the player is
a prince or princess at all. He has not spoken to the player in years. On the day of the
Apportionment, in one of his rare lucid hours, he writes a letter in his own hand and
sends the player a gift: a choice of three legendary Pokémon, **Entei, Raikou or
Suicune**, from the royal kennels. It is the only thing he has written himself in years,
and Chancellor Venn did not see it before it left. When lucid, he is kind, guilty, and
frightened.

## Crownspire (capital)

A walled city on a cliff at the island's centre, built around the Bell Tower. Holds the
throne room, the Royal Archive, the Chancellery and the old cathedral, whose undercroft
holds what the cult calls the Sleeper. Suggested map slot: Ever Grande City, with the
Pokémon League rebuilt as the palace and the Elite Four rooms as the Privy Council
chambers.

Crownspire is closed to the player until Act 4.

### Geography

Lowmere sits at the far south-west edge of the island, in salt marsh, reachable only by
the Mire Road. That road leads to Haymarket, the nearest market town, which controls
what grain and goods reach Lowmere. Every route in the kingdom eventually leads uphill to
Crownspire.

## Factions

- **House Valcourt**: the royal family. Ten heirs, one king.
- **The Chancellery**: the civil service, headed by Chancellor Maelis Venn. Writes the
  king's decrees.
- **The magistrates**: one per town, appointed by the Crown. They hear cases and
  authorise Trials. Some are honest; many answer to the Chancellery.
- **The Gilded Scale**: a merchant guild operating in every town. A front for the Pale
  Choir's money.
- **The Pale Choir**: the cult. See [cult.md](cult.md).
- **The Commons**: ordinary townsfolk. The player's real power base. Each pact wins their
  trust; by the end they are the council that replaces the court.
